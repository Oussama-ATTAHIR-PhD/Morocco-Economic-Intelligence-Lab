# Morocco Household Purchasing Power Study (2007–2024)

## Overview

This study analyses the evolution of Moroccan household purchasing power between 2007 and 2024.

The analysis focuses on the household institutional sector (S.14) and examines the relationship between:

- Disposable income
- Inflation
- Household consumption
- Real income
- Real consumption
- Saving behaviour

The study aims to distinguish nominal economic growth from real purchasing power.

---

## Research Question

How did inflation affect the purchasing power of Moroccan households between 2007 and 2024?

---

## Key Findings

Between 2007 and 2024:

- Consumer prices increased by 36.96%.
- Nominal disposable income increased by 123.91%.
- Nominal household consumption increased by 127.60%.
- Real disposable income increased by 63.48%.
- Real household consumption increased by 66.18%.

The study also identified major turning points:

- Highest inflation: 2022 (6.64%)
- Largest decline in real disposable income: 2020 (-4.35%)
- Strongest real income growth: 2021 (8.09%)
- Highest household saving rate: 2020 (15.20%)
- Lowest household saving rate: 2023 (10.54%)

---

## Main Conclusion

Moroccan households recorded substantial long-term gains in real income and real consumption between 2007 and 2024.

However, the evolution was not linear.

The analysis shows that household purchasing power remains vulnerable to economic shocks and inflationary pressures.

The major inflationary episode of 2022 demonstrated that nominal income growth does not necessarily translate into an improvement in real purchasing power.

---

## Data Sources

The study is based on data from the High Commission for Planning of Morocco (HCP).

Main datasets include:

- Non-financial accounts of households (S.14)
- Household disposable income
- Household final consumption expenditure
- Consumer Price Index

All monetary values are expressed in millions of Moroccan dirhams.

---

## Methodology

Real values are calculated by deflating nominal household aggregates using the harmonized Consumer Price Index.

The study calculates:

- Inflation rate
- Real disposable income
- Real consumption
- Real disposable income growth
- Household saving rate

Limitations

The study analyses macroeconomic aggregates for the household institutional sector.

Therefore, the results should not be interpreted as measuring the purchasing power of every individual household.

Aggregate improvements may differ across:

Income groups
Regions
Urban and rural households
Employment categories
